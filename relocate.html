Why people move here

Most of my relocation clients are moving toward family

My clients come from all over the country, and in my experience the biggest reason is family.

Closer to kids and grandkidsA lot of my clients are parents whose kids came here for college and stayed, and now there are grandkids in the picture.
Coming back homePeople who left for military service or a job elsewhere and want to be back near family.
A fresh startA new job or a growing family, with a new place to call home.

What surprises newcomers

A few things work differently here

None of these are deal breakers, but knowing them ahead of time makes the first few months easier.

01

A lot of places close on Sunday

Many businesses in Utah County are closed on Sundays, including most builders' model homes. If you're flying in to look at new construction, plan your model home visits for Friday and Saturday.

02

Buying alcohol works differently

Most alcohol is sold through state-run liquor stores. Grocery and convenience stores carry only basic beer.

03

Home prices are on the higher end

Utah's typical home value ranks about 6th highest of the 50 states. If you're coming from California or another higher-priced market, that can feel like a break. If you're coming from most other states, expect to pay more than you're used to.

Source: Zillow Home Value Index, August 2026.

04

Negotiating happens after the offer

In a lot of states, buyers negotiate before an offer is accepted. Here, most of the negotiating happens during the due diligence period after you're under contract, which usually runs about 7 to 10 days, with occasional extensions when a deeper inspection is needed.

That makes buying here faster and smoother in a lot of ways, but it can feel rushed if you're used to a slower process. Inspections need to be lined up quickly, which is a big reason to have a local agent on your side.

05

You don't need an attorney to buy

Unlike some East Coast states, Utah doesn't require a lawyer to be part of a home purchase.

06

Big city when you want it

Salt Lake City has the bigger nightlife and city amenities. Just about everything else you'd want is within roughly 30 miles of Point of the Mountain, the Draper and Bluffdale area between Salt Lake and Utah County.

How we'll pick your area

I start with how you live, not with a map

I've helped clients look in counties all over the state, and the right fit is different for everyone. So before I recommend anywhere, I ask about your life now.

Then I send you on a drive. I'll suggest a few cities and communities I think you'll like, give you directions, and ask you to spend a few hours or an afternoon driving around. During your exploration, I will help highlight local amenities and help you get a good feel for the area. Then you tell me what you liked and didn't, and we narrow it down from there.

Questions I'll ask you

  • What do you love about your neighborhood and city right now?
  • What do you wish were different?
  • Do you like the outdoors? Hiking, biking, riding?
  • Do you have a dog you like to take on long walks?
  • Suburban, city, or a quieter rural pace?
  • Where will you be working, and how far are you willing to drive?

Buying from out of state

You can do most of it from home. Come out at least once.

I do FaceTime tours instead of recorded videos. Live video lets me answer questions as we walk through and point out things a video would miss. When you can't be here, I'll keep house hunting with you over FaceTime, and when needed I'll even do contracts while you're out of state.

Still, I don't recommend buying a home you've never seen in person. Video can only do so much, and my opinion isn't yours. If you go under contract from out of state, plan to come see the home in person during the due diligence period to confirm you love it.

Start sooner than you think.

It's always easier to casually look for an extra month or two before you get serious. It's much harder, and a lot more stressful, to reach out the day you land and start looking.

Your scouting weekend

When you can come out for a weekend, I clear my schedule for you. Here's how it usually goes.

  1. Before you fly in

    We pick the areas and the homes

    We talk ahead of time about the areas I think you'll like, then I pull homes in those areas that fit your budget and you choose which ones to see. I'll also have you give each home a nickname before we go. More on that below.

  2. Morning

    Showings in the first area

    A few showings to start the day. I'll have snacks, water, notebooks and pens packed for you, and if anyone needs gas or a bathroom break, just say so and we'll take a detour.

  3. Lunch

    Eat where you might live

    Lunch somewhere nearby is part of the tour. It's an easy way to see what's around the homes you just saw.

  4. Afternoon

    Repeat in a second area

    Same idea in a different area, so you can compare how they feel.

  5. Before you leave

    Revisit or explore

    If you have a few hours on your last day, I'll send you to check out another area I think you'd like, or back through a neighborhood you already loved. Either you find a couple more places worth seeing, or you confirm what you liked the day before.

Why every home gets a nickname

After ten houses in a day, they all start to blur together. A nickname sticks. I once showed a home we called the Cat House because it had cat art and cat wallpaper throughout. Nobody forgot which one that was.

Rent, buy or build

What I usually recommend

Usually best

Buy right away

Renting here isn't always easy. Families with young kids especially have a hard time renting for a year, because they don't want the kids making new friends and settling into a school only to move again.

Often easier

Build new

Unless you have to move right away for work, new construction can make a long-distance move easier. While the home is being built, you have time to get your current home ready to sell.

Portable storage helps too. You can declutter now, ship your things to Utah, and give yourself more breathing room before moving day.

If you need to rent

Rent where you'd buy

If renting first is the right call, I'll help you find a short-term lease, month to month or six months, in one of the communities you like most. You get to actually live there and feel it out, and when you do buy, the move is a lot less dramatic.

Selling a home before you move? Use the sell first or build first countdown to see when to list.

Commute

Know where your office is

Depending on what you do, you may be driving into one of the areas where offices are clustered, and those get heavy traffic around the standard morning and evening rush. The four hot spots to plan around:

  • Pleasant Grove
  • Thanksgiving Point
  • Draper
  • The I-215 belt route

If you'll commute through one of these every day, it's worth factoring into which area you pick.

Schools

Neighborhood first, then schools

I bring up schools once we've found areas you like. In my experience, it's rare for a family to choose a public school over a neighborhood they love. When education is a top priority, families usually look at charter or private schools, and those have almost no boundaries here as long as you're willing to drive.

If you love a home near a district line, we'll compare the nearby schools for what matters to your family. For example, American Fork is known for its music program, and Lone Peak is known for sports like basketball and football.

Utah County school districts

  • Nebo School DistrictSouth Utah County
  • Provo School DistrictProvo
  • Alpine School DistrictNorth and central Utah County, splitting into three new districts (this change goes into effect the 2027–28 school year):
  • Alpine, American Fork, Cedar Hills, Highland, Lehi and the Utah County part of Draper
  • Eagle Mountain, Saratoga Springs, Cedar Fort and Fairfield
  • Orem, Lindon, Pleasant Grove and Vineyard

Seasons

Four real seasons

100ssummer highs, into the low 100s
20swinter lows
±5°difference between the bench and the valley floor

Depending on the year, the valleys along the Wasatch Front can get a lot of snowstorms. Where you live matters too. Homes up on the bench (the higher ground along the mountains) can run about 5 degrees different from homes lower in the valley.

Living on the bench? Get all-wheel or four-wheel drive.

It makes a real difference getting to work or taking the kids to school on snowy mornings before the plows have cleared the roads.

Life here

What I love about living here

Food

There's a wide variety, and you can find most types of food within about 15 to 20 minutes of anywhere along the Wasatch Front.

The outdoors

Five national parks in the state, plus hiking, biking and riding trails within a few minutes of just about anywhere you live.

Pro sports

Utah is home to the Utah Jazz for basketball, the Utah Mammoth for hockey, and Real Salt Lake if you're into soccer.

Golf

Courses all along the Wasatch Front, so you can play pretty much anytime you want.

After you're under contract

The details I handle for you

When you close, I give you the info for your city so setting up utilities is easy.

Two kinds of water. Many Utah homes split water into two systems: potable (drinking) water for inside the home and the hose spigots, and secondary water, which is untreated irrigation water for lawns and landscaping. I always walk new construction buyers through it, and anyone else whose home has it.

HOA rules, summarized. If your community has an HOA, I go through the CC&Rs and pull out the important parts so you know what to expect before you move in.

Building from out of state?

I'll visit your home during construction, take pictures and send you updates, so you know what's happening at every step even from a thousand miles away.

A recent move

All five of their adult kids had moved here for college, and a few had started families. They wanted to be close to the grandkids, in a home big enough to host everyone for the holidays.

  1. First visit. They came out for one weekend, and we saw about 7 homes and talked to builders in a few different areas. They took my advice to focus on the cities and communities they'd actually want to live in.

  2. Second visit. Over the next month we planned a longer showing trip. In 2 days we saw more than 30 homes and visited several builders. At the end of day two, I suggested one last builder I thought they'd like, and they fell in love with a home.

  3. Under contract. Within a week of flying home, we were under contract on a new construction home.

  4. The build. I went by every week to take pictures and send updates, and caught several errors before they turned into big problems.

  5. Moving day. They sold their old home and moved out about a week before we closed on their new one.
